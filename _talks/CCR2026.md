Following the discovery that the infinite Ramsey theorem for pairs and two colors, \\\\(\\mathsf{RT}^2\\\_2\\\\), escapes the structural phenomenon known in reverse mathematics as the “Big Five”, many Ramsey-like statements have been studied from computational and reverse-mathematical perspectives.



In this talk, we consider weakenings of \\\\(\\mathsf{RT}^2\\\_2\\\\) where the graphs under consideration avoid certain patterns. Among these patterns, we show that separable permutations, a well-studied class in combinatorics, play a central role.



In particular, we prove that, for every separable permutation \\\\(p\\\\), the statement \\\\(\\mathsf{coRT}^2\\\_2(p)\\\\), asserting that every infinite \\\\(p\\\\)-avoiding graph has an infinite clique or anti-clique, admits probabilistic solutions, and that \\\\(\\emptyset'\\\\)-\\\\(\\mathsf{DNC}\\\\) degrees are precisely the strength needed to compute them.



This is a joint work with Ludovic Patey.
