The bounded Ramsey theorem for pairs (\\\\(\\mathsf{BRT}^2\\\_2\\\\)) is a weakening of Ramsey's theorem for pairs (\\\\(\\mathsf{RT}^2\\\_2\\\\)), where we have a guarantee that no clique of color 0 for a given size \\\\(k\\\\) exists. This weakening is computably true, in the sense that every computable instance of it has a computable solution, and we show that proving this fact requires \\\\(\\Sigma\\\_2\\\\)-induction. On the other hand, the mere existence of a solution (not necessarily a computable one) is a direct consequence of \\\\(\\mathsf{RT}^2\\\_2\\\\), which is known not imply \\\\(\\mathsf{I}\\Sigma\\\_2\\\\) over \\\\(\\mathsf{RCA}\\\_0\\\\).



This is a joint work with Ludovic Patey.
